    CPDB f² Lifespan Correction

    The calculator applies the CPDB lifespan correction to dose before fitting the response. CPDB describes f as experiment time divided by standard lifespan, with 2 years used for rats, mice, and hamsters. Exposure duration is retained separately for dose reconstruction, but experiment duration is used for f². Applying the correction to dose before fitting is mathematically equivalent to reporting the fitted TD50 multiplied by f² when all rows share the same experiment duration.

    f = experiment time on test / standard lifespan CPDB f² = (experiment weeks / standard lifespan weeks)^2 CPDB-adjusted dose = dose rate x f² reported TD50 = 0.693 / B fitted against CPDB-adjusted dose default standard lifespan = 104 weeks default half-lifespan flag = 52 weeks

    Dose Inputs

    direct dose mode: dose rate is used as entered in mg/kg/day total-dose mode: dose rate = total mg / (body weight kg x exposure weeks x 7) concentration mode: total mg = concentration mg/L x intake L/day x dosing weeks x days/week

    TD50 Method

    P = treated tumor proportion; P0 = control tumor proportion observed over control = (P - P0) / (1 - P0) linear y = -ln(1 - observed over control) B = zero-intercept slope of linear y versus effective dose expected over control = 1 - exp(-B x dose) TD50 = 0.693 / B

    The fit plot shows the linearized form of the one-hit model, not a raw tumor-incidence curve. The points are transformed article responses, the line is the fitted slope B forced through zero, and the TD50 is the dose where that line reaches 0.693.

    Bootstrap Plot And PDF Report

    The bootstrap resamples tumor counts within the entered group sizes using a deterministic seed from the study inputs. It reflects incidence-count uncertainty only; dose selection, pathology calls, survival adjustment, and model-form uncertainty are not included. Export PDF opens the browser print flow so the report can be saved as a PDF on Mac or Windows.

    Why External Database Values May Differ

    External TD50 databases may account for additional study-specific handling that this transparent article-entry calculator does not reproduce: dose-group exclusion or selection, poor-fit flags, intercurrent-mortality or life-table treatment, terminal-sacrifice summary methods, single-concentration rows where a database may not calculate TD50, saturated treated incidences, no dose-response decisions, tumor subtype handling, and database-specific statistical trend tests or confidence intervals.

    Reference And Caveat

    Equation reference: Gaylor and Gold. Quick estimate of the regulatory virtually safe dose based on the maximum tolerated dose for rodent bioassays. Regul Toxicol Pharmacol. 1995;22(1):57-63. This tool estimates from user-entered article data and is not an official CPDB, regulatory, or externally validated calculation.
